Two thresholds, two jobs

Every cell below carries two similarity thresholds, and confusing them ships the wrong one.

yardstick

The TAR@FAR threshold is set by pinning every encoder to the same false-accept budget ({{ '%g'|format(primary_far|float * 100) }}%), so the comparison is fair. It is a measuring instrument for ranking encoders. It is never deployed.

deployment

The max-F1 threshold is the cut-off that best balances precision and recall on the real score distributions. This is the one stored in the gallery and used live.

The curves behind the numbers

Every scalar in the tables above is one point read off a curve. These are the curves — one line per encoder, drawn from the sweep recorded in the same run that produced the table. Drag the threshold and the ring travels along each curve; the table underneath is what you would ship at that setting.

Non-mates outnumber genuine pairs by roughly 50 to 1 here — every probe is scored against every other household gallery, plus {{ '{:,}'.format(run.num_strangers) }} LFW strangers. Precision divides by the accepts you actually made, so it collapses the moment non-mates start getting in; FAR divides by that same large non-mate count, so it flatters everything. That is why the two plots disagree about vgg-face, and why the ROC defaults to a log axis — untick it and all three curves fold into the top-left corner, taking the only region anyone deploys in with them.

Filled dots are the two thresholds from the callout above, at their exact off-grid positions: the TAR@FAR yardstick (which is why the amber dots sit at FAR 1%), and the max-F1 threshold that ships. The hollow ring is wherever you have dragged the slider. AP and AUC in the legend are the exact full-resolution values — the same numbers as the columns above, not integrals of the drawn line. Click an encoder to hide it.
