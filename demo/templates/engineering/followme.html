A follow-me drone has to find a person — and the car or motorbike they get into — from the air, live, on a laptop. Off-the-shelf detectors are trained on photos taken at head height and barely work from a drone. This page is the record of fixing that, one controlled experiment at a time.

Every model below starts from the same YOLOv8n pretrained on COCO, with the same recipe and seed; only the training data changes. Scores are mAP50 over eight classes (person, bicycle, car, van, truck, bus, motorcycle, three-wheeler) from a scorer that, like the VisDrone toolkit, does not count a detection against the model when it lands in a region the dataset marked as unlabeled.

Decisions

01

Stock YOLO doesn't work from the air, so the data is the project

Stock YOLOv8n scores {{ '%.3f'|format(v('stock','visdrone')[0]) }} on VisDrone test-dev. Fine-tuned on VisDrone it scores {{ '%.2f'|format(v('vd_gray','visdrone')[0]) }}.

From above, cars are roofs and people are heads and shoulders, and most objects are a few pixels across. That is domain shift, and the only real fix is training on footage that looks like what the drone sees. But the same chart shows the price: the fine-tuned model scores {{ '%.3f'|format(v('vd_gray','coco')[0]) }} on ordinary COCO photos, down from {{ '%.3f'|format(v('stock','coco')[0]) }}. That is decision 03.

02

Two datasets thrown out for their labels, not their size

I reviewed eleven aerial datasets and kept two. AU-AIR and JATAYU were cut after I looked at their labels by hand.

AU-AIR leaves parked and distant cars unlabeled and boxes whole rows of cars as one. JATAYU has a median of two boxes per image, including busy traffic scenes with dozens of vehicles. A missing label teaches the model that a real car is background, so these datasets would punish it for being right — more data, worse model. The two keepers were checked the same way: VisDrone has a median of 64 labels per image, MAVREC 21. {% if followme.excluded_test_sets %} For the same reason, JATAYU's test set is left out of every score on this page: a score against missing labels doesn't measure anything. {% endif %}

03

The model forgot how to see normally, and COCO fixed it

Mixing COCO images back into training takes COCO from {{ '%.3f'|format(v('vd_gray','coco')[0]) }} to {{ '%.3f'|format(v('vd_coco_l','coco')[0]) }}, while VisDrone stays between {{ '%.3f'|format(v('vd_coco_l','visdrone')[0]) }} and {{ '%.3f'|format(v('vd_coco_m','visdrone')[0]) }}.

Fine-tuning on drone footage alone left a model that only predicted tiny boxes — fatal for following someone at four metres, where a person fills the frame. That's catastrophic forgetting. Retraining on a mix fixes most of it at no cost to the drone score. Returns diminish: going from 1.6k to 6.5k COCO images gains more than going from 6.5k to 13k, which is why the dataset experiments below use 6.5k and only the final model gets 13k.

04

How unlabeled crowds are handled turned out not to matter

Dropped, grayed out, or masked in the loss: three seeds each, and the three land within the seed spread of each other.

VisDrone marks crowds it didn't label as "ignored regions". Dropping them (the default) punishes the model for finding real people there; graying the pixels hides them; masking them in the loss — which Ultralytics doesn't support, so I wrote and verified it — neither punishes nor rewards. The textbook answer is the loss mask. On this data it makes no measurable difference, so I stopped spending time on it. The error bars are the spread across seeds, and they are why I ran three.

05

Drone datasets don't transfer to each other — so train on both

Trained on MAVREC alone, the model scores {{ '%.3f'|format(v('mav_cm','visdrone')[0]) }} on VisDrone — worse than stock. Trained on both, it keeps nearly all of each.

VisDrone is Chinese cities at many heights; MAVREC is European scenes at 25–45 m. Each model learns its own camera height and scenery. Combining them costs a little on VisDrone overall but gives the best person score of any model ({{ '%.3f'|format(v('vd_mav_cm','visdrone','person')[0]) }}). My drone is a third domain neither dataset has seen, so variety is the safer bet — and my own footage is the test that settles it. MAVREC's own test scores are optimistic: its official train and test splits share the same scenes.

07

Training curves: not overfitting, a little underfit

{% if not curves %}

Training curves unavailable: results/followme_training_curves.json is missing. Run python tools/export_training_curves.py RUNS_DIR.

{% else %}

In every run the validation loss keeps falling to the last epoch and ends close to the training loss. That rules out overfitting and points the other way: the smallest YOLO is close to the limit of what it can learn from this data.

What the three losses measure

A detector is graded on three things at once, and Ultralytics logs each as its own loss:

  • Box loss (CIoU): how far each predicted box is from the real one. It combines how much they overlap, how far apart their centres are and how different their shapes are. Lower means boxes sit tighter on objects.
  • Class loss (binary cross-entropy): at every place the model could put a box, how wrong its class scores are. That includes saying "nothing here" over plain background. This is where the confidence score comes from, so it is also the loss behind the cutoff question. Lower means the right class with high confidence, and low confidence on background.
  • DFL (distribution focal loss): YOLOv8 predicts each box edge as a probability spread over nearby positions instead of a single number. DFL pushes that spread to be sharp around the true edge, so lower means more precise edges.

The total is the sum of the three, each multiplied by its weight (box 7.5, class 0.5, DFL 1.5, the Ultralytics defaults). Training loss is measured on the augmented training images the model is learning from. Validation loss is measured on clean images it never trains on.

Reading the curves

Overfitting looks like validation loss bottoming out and then climbing while training loss keeps falling: the model is memorising its training images. Underfitting looks like both losses staying high and close together, or still falling when training stops: there is more to learn, or the model is too small to learn it. These runs show three things.

  1. No overfitting. Validation loss (dashed) falls in every run all the way to epoch 50, and the best checkpoint by mAP50-95 lands at epoch {% for r in curves.runs %}{{ r.best_epoch }}{% if not loop.last %}, {% endif %}{% endfor %} respectively, at or near the end. A model that had started memorising would peak earlier and decline.
  2. Train and validation end close together. Early on, training loss sits above validation because mosaic augmentation (four images stitched into one, rescaled) makes training images harder than the clean validation ones. The dotted line marks where mosaic switches off for the last ten epochs, and training loss drops visibly there. Even after that, eight of the ten runs end with validation loss within about 0.13 of training loss, and the three final candidates within 0.13, 0.02 and -0.03. A model with spare capacity fits its own training data far better than new data; these barely do, which is what a 3.2M-parameter nano model looking at objects a few pixels across looks like. That is the "too small" kind of underfitting, and it is the reason for the model race, not more epochs. Two runs pull further apart. VisDrone + 1.6k COCO (0.38) is validated partly on COCO photos it saw too few of to learn. MAVREC + 6.5k COCO (0.27) separates only in the last ten epochs, the closest any run comes to overfitting, though its validation loss is still falling.
  3. Still improving, but flattening. Both losses are still falling slowly at epoch 50, so more epochs would help a little. The mAP chart for the featured run shows how little: most of the gain is in the first 20 epochs.

Two things that look odd but aren't problems. The runs with the bigger data mixes ({{ curves.runs|selectattr('optimizer','equalto','MuSGD')|list|length }} of {{ curves.runs|length }}) were given MuSGD at learning rate 0.01 by optimizer=auto, and their loss bumps up over epochs 2–3 while the warmup raises the learning rate. The VisDrone-only runs got AdamW at about 0.0008 and have no bump. Each run is validated on its own training mix's validation set, so compare the shape of the curves across runs, not their height.

Video it never saw: the drone mix wins from the air and holds up on the ground

{% if not unseen %}

Unseen-video scores unavailable: results/followme_unseen_video.json is missing.

{% else %}

mAP rewards a model across every confidence. A drone has to pick one cutoff and live with it, so here each model is scored the way it would fly: F1 at its own frozen cutoff, on video none of them trained on.

  • Cutoffs come from validation only. Each model gets the person and vehicle confidence that maximizes the equal-weight average F1 over VisDrone val, MAVREC val and the COCO holdout. Equal weight means no one altitude dominates. The cutoff is then frozen; no test set ever moves it.
  • VisDrone-MOT is drone video from the air, people and vehicles. MOT17 and MOT20 are street-level crowds, people only. They check that the drone training didn't break the near-ground view a follow-me drone also sees at low altitude.
  • Labels checked first. {{ unseen.doc.label_check }}
{% for c in unseen.charts %}{{ chart(c) }}{% endfor %}

Reading it: off the shelf, stock YOLOv8n is fine on street crowds (MOT17 F1 0.58) and weak from the air (VisDrone-MOT person 0.32). VisDrone alone flips that: better from the air, worse on MOT17 (0.42). Mixing COCO back in fixes the ground view, and the VisDrone + MAVREC + COCO mixes are best from the air. VD+MAV+COCO-L has the best MOT17 score (0.63) and is within about 0.01 of the best on VisDrone-MOT, which is why it's the locked recipe for the model race. It isn't best everywhere: VD + COCO-M leads on the densest crowds (MOT20 0.68 vs 0.63). MAVREC + COCO alone collapses on dense crowds (MOT20 0.30): MAVREC's sparse scenes don't teach crowds.

09

On my own drone: the winner, quantized to INT8, running at 27 FPS on a laptop

Flown outside on Oct 29 with the Parrot ANAFI 4K. The winning detector, quantized to INT8 with OpenVINO, plus BoT-SORT with camera-motion compensation, ran live on the laptop CPU at 27.4 FPS (detector 35 ms median) for 805 seconds.

  • What the clips are: 30-second cuts of that flight's recording, re-run through the same INT8 detector, tracker and follow controller. The banner shows the controller's state (SEARCHING / LOCKED), the locked target, the estimated distance and the turn / forward / tilt it would command; the bottom bar shows height, gimbal angle and frame rate.
  • INT8 cost almost nothing: calibrated on 300 training images, it lost 0.01 F1 and 0.004 mAP50 and ran about 3× faster (detector + BoT-SORT 95 ms → 26–29 ms per frame).
  • The controller is deliberately simple: proportional control on three axes (gimbal, yaw, forward/back to hold about 6 m), with hover on a lost or stale target and any key handing control back to me. Tuning it is the next step.
