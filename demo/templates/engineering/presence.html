It has been verified end to end against a real MQTT broker from a video file; the live-camera path is written and has never executed. The pipeline design, unmeasured

Six stages. The only one that is not obvious is the second: frames arrive faster than they can be processed, and what happens to the excess decides whether the answer is about now or about the recent past.

Why the buffer overwrites

The worker cannot keep up with the camera, and it never will — detection and embedding together dominate the frame budget. So something has to give, and the choice is between dropping the excess and queueing it.

Queueing fails in the worst possible way: silently. A backlog does not announce itself. The service keeps answering, every answer stays well-formed, and each one is computed from a frame slightly older than the last until the lag is measured in minutes. A presence answer derived from a twelve-second-old frame is not a late answer — it is a wrong one, and nothing in the output distinguishes it from a correct one.

Overwriting bounds staleness at a single frame no matter how far behind the worker falls, and the cost is a number the service counts and reports rather than hides. The alternative anyone reaches for first — process every Nth frame — is worse than both: it assumes a processing time that is not constant, varies with how many faces are in shot, and drifts with machine load.

Why two topic branches

People are published to jarvis/presence/<name>. The service's own liveness is published somewhere else entirely, to jarvis/status/presence_service, and the separation is not cosmetic tidiness.

MQTT wildcards do not honour naming conventions. A subscriber on jarvis/presence/+ receives every child of that level, and a broker has no notion of a leading underscore meaning "internal" — so a status topic parked inside the presence branch arrives at every consumer looking exactly like one more person. Moving it out is what makes jarvis/presence/+ safely mean "all people".

The status topic also resolves an ambiguity the presence topics cannot. A retained absent means either "nobody is there" or "this service died and what you are reading is a fossil". offline is registered as the MQTT last will, so the broker announces it if the process is killed; a clean shutdown suppresses the will, so the service publishes it itself before disconnecting.

What would make this measured

Three things, none of them done:

  • Run it on a live camera. Everything reported here came from a video file. Device open, native blocking rate and driver buffering are all untested.
  • Justify N, M and T against recorded footage. They currently rest on an argument, and an argument is not a measurement. Detection and recognition each earned their thresholds from a benchmark with a timestamp; this stage has no equivalent.
  • Measure the false-transition rate. The debounce exists to suppress flapping, and nobody has counted how often it flaps.
