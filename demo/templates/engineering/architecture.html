the destination

A system that learns a household's behavioural patterns from multimodal sensor streams and predicts what happens next — rather than reporting what just happened.

Everything measured elsewhere in this log — detection, enrollment, recognition — is infrastructure for that. It is worth being precise about the difference: the face pipeline is not a small version of the goal, it is the sensor layer underneath it.

This is the argument. The evidence was the rest of the log. Nothing on this page has been built or measured: no runs, no tables, no numbers of its own. The few figures it quotes are read from the artifacts behind the pages that earned them, and appear only to mark how much of the road has actually been walked. This page sits last on purpose — the case for a system like this is worth exactly as much as the discipline of the work underneath it, which is why that comes first. When anything here acquires evidence, it stops being architecture and moves onto a page that can defend itself.

The ladder direction, not findings

Each stage of this project raises the level of abstraction the system operates at. That is the whole shape of the thing: pixels become faces, faces become identities, identities become presence, presence becomes pattern — and pattern, eventually, becomes anticipation. The solid segment is measured and shipped. The dashed segment is not built.

It is also a one-dimensional summary of something that is not one dimensional. Everything above presence collapses into two rungs here, and inside those two rungs are two separate memory systems, a decision layer, and a feedback loop. The rest of this page opens that up.

The pipeline is a sensor, not the system direction

A recognizer answers one question, once: who is this. That is a stateless verdict about a single frame, and on its own it can only ever produce a log — Michael was seen at 18:42, and again at 18:43, and again at 18:44. Nothing in that sequence knows it is describing one evening.

Presence events are the first thing in the stack with a time axis, and that is what makes them the real output of everything measured in this log. A stream of "who is where, when" is a behavioural record. Once the household has a behavioural record, the interesting question stops being what did the camera see and becomes what does this household usually do, and is right now unusual.

That is the reframe the whole project turns on. The face pipeline is worth building carefully — hence the thresholds, the open-set benchmarks, the curve explorers — precisely because everything above it inherits its errors. A predictor trained on a noisy presence stream learns the noise.

Why JEPA unproven at this scale

Predicting a hidden representation from visible context is structurally the same problem as predicting future behaviour from partial observation.

A joint-embedding predictive architecture is trained to take what it can see, and predict the representation of what it cannot — not the raw missing pixels, but the abstract encoding of them. The distinction is the entire point. Reconstructing pixels forces a model to spend its capacity on the exact fall of a shadow and the texture of a carpet, detail that is genuinely unpredictable and worth nothing. Predicting in representation space lets it be right about what is going on while remaining agnostic about the pixels that would express it.

Behaviour has exactly that shape. At 18:40 on a weekday the house has partial observation: someone came through the front door, the kitchen light is on, no one has been upstairs for an hour. The useful prediction is not a frame of video from 18:50. It is the representation of the next interval — that this is the evening-meal pattern rather than the heading-out-again pattern — which is precisely the object a JEPA is trained to produce.

The masking that makes the training signal is also free here, and that matters more than it sounds. In vision you have to invent the occlusion. A house supplies it: sensors have blind spots, people leave the frame, radar sees through a wall the camera cannot, a wearable keeps reporting from a room with no camera in it. Every gap in coverage is a training example in disguise — predict the hidden modality's representation from the visible ones.

Which is why the modalities matter more than any single model. Camera, radar and wearables do not just add coverage; they give each other supervision. Each one's blind spot is another one's label.

The modalities this would have to train on are novel ones — radar point clouds, vitals time-series — not images. There is no pretrained backbone to start from and no published recipe to copy, which is a real part of the difficulty and a real part of why it is interesting.

Two memory systems architecture, unbuilt

The obvious mistake would be to pick one. A vector store alone cannot tell you why; a knowledge graph alone cannot notice a similarity nobody ever wrote down. The design keeps both, because they fail in opposite directions and each one covers the other's blind spot.

distributed · geometric

Embedding space

JEPA-family self-supervised encoders over raw sensor streams — mmWave radar point clouds, Oura vitals time-series — joint-embedded so that "Michael's body in state X" lands in one location regardless of which sensor saw it. Similarity is geometry: two days that resemble each other are near each other, whether or not anyone ever described either day.

can't say why. It knows two Tuesdays rhyme. It has no account of what a Tuesday is.

explicit · declarative · relational

Knowledge graph

Entities extracted by the LLM as nodes and edges — people, places, commitments, causes. Its job is relational traversal, not keyword recall: following a chain of reasoning across months of conversations to reach a fact that was never stated in any single one of them.

can't notice what was never said. Every node in it got there because something articulated it.

No pretrained mmWave foundation model exists to download. That is not an obstacle to route around — it is why training the backbone is the legitimate piece of work here rather than a detour on the way to one.

How they are wired — eight paths architecture, unbuilt

Two memories and a decision layer are only a design once you say how information moves between them. Eight paths, each named, grouped by what they do: four write into memory, two read out of it, one closes the loop from outcomes, and one runs deferred while nothing else is happening.

write
  1. Transcript conversation into the graph — entities, relations, commitments, as they are said.
  2. Resonance clustering in the embedding space discovers graph nodes that no single conversation ever stated. Geometry proposes a concept; the graph gets a node for it. This is the path that makes two memories worth more than either alone, and it is the one a reader is least likely to guess.
  3. Perception raw sensor streams into the embedding space — the encoder's day job.
  4. Reflex the fast write: what lands in memory without waiting for anything to deliberate about it first.
read
  1. Reasoning traversal of the graph — following relations to an answer.
  2. Intuition nearest-neighbour recall out of the embedding space — "this looks like that", before anything can say why.
feedback
  1. Truth the Observer. What actually happened, returned to memory, so a prediction that missed is recorded as having missed.
deferred
  1. Reflection consolidation between the two memories, run when nothing else needs the machine.

The Conductor decides. The LLM speaks. architecture, unbuilt

The decision layer is a separate component from the language model, and keeping them separate is the point rather than an implementation detail. The Conductor decides whether to act, when, how — and, most of the time, whether to stay silent. The LLM is a language engine: given that something should be said, it is very good at saying it.

Collapsing the two is the default mistake in assistant design, and it produces a system that talks because talking is what it does. An LLM asked "should I say something here?" will find a reason often enough to be exhausting. Silence has to be a decision made somewhere that is not rewarded for being articulate.

The loop that makes it falsifiable architecture, unbuilt

A behavioural system that learns from feedback can drift into telling itself a nice story, because it grades its own homework. The design forecloses that with a commitment made in advance.

1
Commit. The Conductor picks one intervention and writes down the outcome it predicts — before running it.
2
Run it. One experiment at a time, so the result is attributable to something.
3
Debrief. The next cycle checks the prediction against what the Observer recorded, and it either held or it did not.

The loop runs at five cadences, nightly through annual — the short ones catch what moved this week, the long ones catch the drift no single week is big enough to show.

Writing the prediction down first is the whole mechanism. A system that only evaluates after the fact can always find a reading of events that flatters it; one that committed in advance can be wrong in a way it cannot talk its way out of.

Design commitments decided, unbuilt

Positions taken in advance, each because getting it wrong later is far more expensive than getting it right now.

Backbone stable, heads adaptive

The encoder stays frozen. Retraining it moves the space, and the moment the space moves, every stored embedding and every graph edge built on top of it is silently wrong — not broken loudly, just quietly no longer meaning what it meant. Retrains are versioned events with a migration, never something that happens in the background.

Privacy is the representation, not a policy

A JEPA keeps the gist and discards the raw. Audio and video are embedded and thrown away — not retained under a promise of good behaviour, not encrypted at rest and kept anyway. The architecture cannot leak footage it never stored, which makes the representation choice itself the guarantee.

This project is data-history-bound

Not skill-bound. The models can be learned later; the months of household history cannot. Every day not collecting is permanently lost — which is the real argument for building the boring capture layer first and why the sensor work came before anything clever.

Rules ship before learning

A rule-based safety layer goes in before any learned coaching does. Learned behaviour is the part that can surprise you, and the floor underneath it has to be one that cannot.

Classic ML has three permanent roles

Not a stepping stone to be outgrown. A dumb-model floor is mandatory in every benchmark — if a constant beats the clever thing, that is the finding. Gradient-boosted trees are the genuine v1 prediction engine, not a placeholder. And interpretable regression is kept forever, because self-insight has to be explainable to be worth anything.

It mirrors a brain-systems decomposition

Loosely, and the metaphor is not evidence for anything: embedding space ~ cortical and hippocampal representation, knowledge graph ~ declarative memory, sleep-triggered consolidation ~ hippocampal-cortical replay. It is retrospective rather than derived — but it explains the entire design in one sentence, which is worth saying out loud.

The nearest concrete step research

None of the above is buildable in one move, and the first real step is already named on the roadmap: use JEPA to train mmWave radar to project into the same embedding space the camera encoder already produces. It is the smallest honest version of the whole thesis — two modalities, one shared representation, learned by predicting one from the other — and it is where the novel modality enters, which is why the sensor track on the ladder widens exactly here.

camera → shared embedding space ← mmWave radar

If it works, a person is identifiable from radar alone, matched against the gallery that already exists, with no separate enrolment — and liveness comes free, because a photograph held up to a camera has no radar signature.

It is also the step that proves or kills the approach cheaply. A shared space between two sensors is a small, checkable claim with an existing gallery to test against. If two modalities in one house cannot be made to agree on a representation, the version with wearables and a time axis was never going to.

What would have to be true open questions

The rest of this log earns its conclusions by saying what would overturn them. A goal deserves the same treatment, so here is what this one is resting on — each of these is currently an assumption, not a finding.

One household is enough data

JEPAs are trained at internet scale. A house produces one stream, from a handful of people, and the routine that makes behaviour predictable is the same property that makes the dataset small and repetitive. It may simply be too little signal to learn a representation from.

Radar and camera can share a space

The nearest step assumes two very different sensors can be pushed into one representation at this scale, on this hardware. That is an open research question and is labelled as one on the roadmap, not a scheduled feature.

Prediction is worth more than reporting

A system that anticipates is only better than one that reports if acting early beats acting correctly. A wrong prediction is more annoying than a late fact, so the bar is not accuracy in the abstract — it is accuracy high enough that acting on it is an improvement.

The sensor layer is clean enough

Everything above the pipeline inherits its errors. The thresholds locked elsewhere in this log were chosen with that in mind, but they were chosen against benchmarks, not against a live house at three in the morning.

Deliberate limits design intent

A system that models a household's behaviour is worth being explicit about. The intent is a house that anticipates the people who live in it and have agreed to it — a household of consenting adults, on hardware they own, with the model and the gallery staying on it. Enrollment is already opt-in by construction: someone has to put photos in a folder. The prediction layer is meant to inherit that property rather than quietly outgrow it, and saying so now is cheaper than retrofitting it later.

Anticipation is the destination. The shared embedding space is the mechanism.

Everything on this page is argued. Everything that is measured is back at the start of the log, where it can be checked — which is the order these two things belong in.
